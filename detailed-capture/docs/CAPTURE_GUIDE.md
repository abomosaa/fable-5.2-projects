# Capture guide

**Good subjects:** flat or nearly flat, static, textured — paintings, posters, prints, documents, maps, murals,
mosaics, fabric laid flat, engraved plates.

**Poor subjects (expect warnings, masks or rejections):** anything with depth photographed from changing
viewpoints (sculpture, relief, objects on a table), glass and glossy varnish with reflections, transparencies,
blank walls, strictly repeating patterns without landmarks, scenes that move or whose lighting changes.

## Steps

1. **Overview.** Fill the frame with the whole subject, square-on, in even light. Tap to focus. Avoid glare: if a
   light source reflects in the surface, move it or yourself. This photo defines the geometry and colour of the
   result, so make it the best one.
2. **Pick an area** on the capture map (tap). The dashed box is roughly what one close-up covers.
3. **Close-ups.** Move closer until the area fills the frame — 2× to 4× closer per step is ideal. Keep the phone
   roughly parallel to the surface (a few degrees of tilt are fine). Overlap each close-up with its neighbours by
   about one third. Use *Lock exposure* so colours stay consistent, and keep the lens (do not switch between
   wide and tele mid-way unless you want to: the app groups frames per lens).
4. **Watch the map.** Green = verified detail (estimate from accepted footprints and measured sharpness). Dashed
   amber/red outlines = uncertain/rejected frames. Amber "+" = largest remaining gaps.
5. **Going very close (5× and more).** Take an intermediate ring first (≈ 2–3×), then the very close frames
   inside it. Very close frames are placed through their overlap with the intermediate ones.
6. **Fuse**, zoom, compare with the overview, check *Sources*. Add or retake frames where needed and fuse again;
   unchanged tiles are reused.

## When a frame is rejected or uncertain

| Message | What to do |
|---|---|
| Not enough reliable matching detail | Step back for an intermediate shot that shows the area with context; make sure it is in focus. |
| Not connected to the overview | The frame matches other close-ups only. Add a frame that links this group to verified area. |
| Repeating pattern / more than one position fits | Include a unique landmark (edge, label, flaw) in an intermediate frame, or use *Place manually* with ≥ 4 point pairs. |
| Blurred / adds no detail | Hold steadier, let focus settle, add light; do not rely on digital zoom. |
| Does not look like the overview there | Wrong area, or the scene changed (moved object, different page). |
| Parts do not agree with a flat surface | Depth, curl, reflection or movement. Flatten the subject, shoot more squarely, remove reflections. Masked parts fall back to the overview. |
| Highlights clipped / glare | Lower exposure or change the angle; clipped areas contain no recoverable detail. |

## Manual placement

Frame → *Place manually*: tap a point in the close-up, then the same point in the overview; repeat for at least
four points spread over the frame. The app refines the placement automatically if it finds matching detail around
your points and otherwise uses the points as given (flagged "manual").

## Honest expectations

* Close-ups supply the detail. Areas without a verified close-up show the enlarged overview and are labelled so.
* Blur, noise, clipped highlights and JPEG artefacts in a close-up end up in the result; nothing is invented to
  hide them. Prefer retaking.
* "Verified" means consistent with the overview at the overview's resolution, not perfect: where one photo ends
  and another begins, very fine edges can step by a pixel or two, and sharpness drops abruptly where close-up
  coverage ends. On subjects with depth, small displaced parts can survive inside a verified photo.
* The result follows the overview's geometry. If the overview is tilted, use *Perspective* in the viewer.
* Detail is not measurement accuracy. Distances are pixels unless you provide a reference length.
