## Known limitations per source

### European-Mediterranean Seismological Centre (EMSC) — SeismicPortal FDSN event web service – real-time earthquake catalogue (EMSC-RTS) (`emsc-earthquakes`)

- **Coverage:** Global for M4+ (the adapter's query); the catalogue is densest in the Euro-Mediterranean region.; The seven days before each poll; older events persist locally until retention expires.
- **Upstream cadence:** Continuously, within minutes of an earthquake (responses are cacheable for 15 seconds); polled every 5 min, flagged stale after 30 min
- **Confidence:** The feed carries no quality or review flag, so records have no confidence value. Severity is derived by this application from magnitude (M4/5/6/7 → minor/moderate/severe/extreme), the same thresholds as the USGS layer.
- **Retention:** Events are kept for 45 days after they occurred, then purged.
- **Licence notes:** Stated on the service documentation page: "The data provided by this service is distributed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0) license."
- **Bundled fixture:** Captured from the live SeismicPortal FDSN event service (format=json, minmag=4, start=2026-11-03) and trimmed from 248 to 138 events: every M5+ event plus every second smaller one; metadata.count adjusted to match. The malformed variant was authored by hand from the same schema.
- Real-time solutions are preliminary: location, depth and magnitude are revised as more stations report, and the contributing agency of the preferred solution can change.
- Only events of magnitude 4 and larger are requested; smaller events are not shown by this source.
- Magnitude types differ between events (mb, ML, Mw, unspecified 'm') and are not strictly comparable.
- The service gives no review-status flag, so automatic and analyst-reviewed solutions cannot be told apart.

- **Licence notes:** Every item of the RSS feed carries "Copyright notice: European Union (CC BY 4.0), reuse is allowed, provided appropriate credit to GDACS is given." (the channel element itself says "public domain"). - Each event is shown at a single centroid point; cyclone tracks, flood extents and drought areas are not included.
- The main feed lists only the earthquakes GDACS currently notifies (a small subset of all earthquakes); population exposure is available only for events present in that feed.
- The 'current' flag is taken from GDACS as is and is occasionally inconsistent with the event text (e.g. long droughts).

### NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information (NCEI) — International Best Track Archive for Climate Stewardship (IBTrACS) v04r01 – ACTIVE storms (`ibtracs`)

- **Coverage:** Global – all tropical cyclone basins.; Storms active within the past 7 days, each with its full track since genesis.
- **Upstream cadence:** Provisional tracks are refreshed about daily; the full v04r01 archive three times a week; polled every 6 h, flagged stale after 4 d
- **Confidence:** IBTrACS gives no per-storm confidence value; provisional tracks are labelled as such in the attributes. Severity is derived by this application from the highest sustained wind along the track: below 34 kt minor, 34–63 kt moderate, 64–95 kt severe, 96 kt and above extreme.
- **Retention:** A track is kept while the storm is in the ACTIVE list (7 days after its last fix) and for 30 days after its last fix.
- **Licence notes:** The product page describes IBTrACS as global, open and publicly reviewed, and asks users to cite Knapp et al. - Wind speeds from different agencies use different averaging periods (1-minute for U.S. agencies, 10-minute for most WMO centres) and are not strictly comparable; the adapter reports the WMO value when present and otherwise the U.S. agency value.

- **Coverage:** Global, but uneven: wildfires come mainly from U.S. interagency reporting (IRWIN) and GDACS, icebergs only from the Antarctic.; Events with a dated position in the past 30 days (open or closed) plus every still-open volcano, iceberg and storm event regardless of age.

- **Confidence:** EONET gives no confidence or quality value, so records carry none. - **Upstream cadence:** Within about three hours of each satellite overpass; polled every 1 h, flagged stale after 6 h

- Outside Europe the 45 km global model is known to over- or under-estimate particulates in desert-dust and biomass-burning regions; extreme values (US AQI above 300) should be checked against local station networks.
- Only a fixed sample of 157 cities is polled, every 3 hours, to stay within the free tier's call limit.
- The US AQI is defined on 24-hour (PM) and 8-hour (ozone) averages; Open-Meteo computes it from rolling model averages, so it is indicative rather than an official index value.

### IODA – Internet Outage Detection and Analysis (Internet Intelligence Lab, Georgia Institute of Technology) — IODA HTTP API v2 – country-level outage events (`ioda`)

- **Coverage:** Global, country level (IODA also publishes sub-national region and AS-level events, which are not ingested).; Events active during the past 7 days, including events that started up to 14 days before that window and were still active in it.
- **Upstream cadence:** Signals are evaluated continuously (BGP visibility every 5 minutes, active probing of each /24 block at least every 10 minutes); events appear and grow with a lag of minutes to a few hours; polled every 15 min, flagged stale after 2 h
- **Confidence:** IODA measures drops in three independent connectivity signals per country: BGP (number of /24 blocks visible to more than 50 % of full-feed Route Views / RIPE RIS peers), active probing (number of /24 blocks responding to Trinocular-style pings) and a network telescope (unique source IPs seen at the Merit darknet). The upstream score is IODA's 'alert area': relative drop in percent multiplied by the outage length in minutes. Confidence is derived by this application from how many of these data sources report overlapping events for the same country (IODA: 'we use multiple datasources to gain confidence about an outage'): one source 0.4, two 0.65, three or more 0.85. Severity is derived from the mean relative drop (score ÷ minutes) and the duration: extreme ≥ 70 % for ≥ 3 h, severe ≥ 40 % for ≥ 1 h, moderate ≥ 10 % for ≥ 30 min, minor ≥ 2 %, otherwise info. These are possible outage signals, not confirmed outages or government-ordered shutdowns.
- **Retention:** Events are kept for 30 days after they ended, then purged. Events that IODA no longer lists (re-segmented or withdrawn) are closed by the pipeline.
- **Licence notes:** As of 2026-11-10 neither the IODA dashboard (home, help, about/acknowledgements pages), nor the API documentation states a licence, terms of use or citation requirement for the data; the API is public and needs no key. The IODA software (github.com/InetIntel/ioda-api, ioda-ui) is released by the Regents of the University of California for academic research and education purposes only, which covers the code, not the data. Because no reuse terms are published, redistribution is recorded as unknown and the source is conservatively flagged non-commercial; credit IODA / Georgia Tech and contact ioda-info@cc.gatech.edu before any commercial or bulk reuse.
- **Bundled fixture:** Captured from the live IODA API (GET /v2/outages/events?from=1793728579&until=1794333379&entityType=country&limit=2000&ignoreMethods=*.sarima) and kept in full: 196 country-level events of the preceding 7 days from the bgp, merit-nt and ping-slash24 data sources, four of them still active at the query time (their duration is clipped at `until`), a few for territories that are not in the gazetteer (GI, GP, RE, TK). The capture was made without an explicit page parameter; page=0 returns the same list. The malformed variant was authored by hand from the same schema (including a second data source overlapping a real Venezuelan event to exercise the source-agreement confidence).
- Events are produced by automated change detection on aggregate signals: IODA raises an alert when a signal falls below a fraction of the median of a sliding window of recent values. A drop can be caused by measurement artefacts, routing changes, maintenance or geolocation changes as well as by a real outage; IODA describes itself as an operational prototype.
- Each record is one event from one data source (bgp, ping-slash24 or merit-nt). The same incident seen by several sources appears as several records for the same country; agreement between sources is expressed in the confidence value.

- **Retention:** Annual values of the last seven years are requested; values are purged 8 years after their reference year.
- **Licence notes:** A small number of WDI series carry third-party restrictions; the indicators used here are CC BY 4.0.
- **Bundled fixture:** Captured from the live World Bank Indicators API with mrv=3 (three most recent years, all economies and aggregates). The malformed variant was authored by hand.
- Annual statistics published with a lag of one to three years; the most recent year is frequently missing for some countries.
- Values are national aggregates and hide sub-national variation.
- Several series are modelled estimates (e.g. ILO unemployment, UN IGME mortality) rather than direct measurements.
- The API intermittently answers individual indicator requests with HTTP 502 or stalls; a failed indicator is retried and the source is shown as delayed (partially refreshed) meanwhile.

### CelesTrak (Dr. T.S. Kelso) — GP element sets (OMM JSON) – space stations, navigation, weather and Earth-resources satellites (`celestrak`)

- **Coverage:** Global (Earth-orbiting objects: about 20 space-station objects, 170 navigation, 75 weather and 165 Earth-resources satellites).; Current computed position only; element sets are typically 0–3 days old.
- **Upstream cadence:** CelesTrak checks for new GP data once every 2 hours; individual element sets are updated 2–3 times a day at most; polled every 2 min, flagged stale after 8 h
- **Confidence:** The mapped position is derived by this application: it is the SGP4 sub-satellite point computed from published mean elements, so 'geometry' is always marked as derived. SGP4 accuracy degrades with element age – the element epoch and its age in hours are attached to every record – and confidence is derived from that age alone: up to 2 days 0.9, up to 7 days 0.7, up to 14 days 0.5, older 0.3. It does not account for manoeuvres after the element epoch.
- **Retention:** One current position per object; the pipeline keeps a short track of recent positions. Records are purged 2 days after their last computed position.
- **Licence notes:** CelesTrak states that it makes these resources freely available to all users but publishes no licence text or redistribution terms; this application therefore shows only positions it computes itself and links back to CelesTrak instead of redistributing the element files. Usage policy (2026-05): download only what is needed, at most once per update – GP data update once every 2 hours; machine clients must stop querying on any non-200 response (301/403/404/5xx) and report to a human; more than 50 such errors in 2 hours or excessive bandwidth gets the IP address firewalled. This adapter therefore caches every group for 7,500 s, never retries a failed request, and after any upstream error works only from its cached element sets for 2 hours (every 2 minutes it merely re-propagates the cached elements).
- **Bundled fixture:** Captured from the live CelesTrak GP API (FORMAT=json) on 2026-11-10 and trimmed to at most 40 objects per group (stations: all 19, including ISS (ZARYA) and CSS (TIANHE)). Element epochs are as captured (2026-11-09/10), i.e. under two days old at the fixture epoch. Positions are computed by the adapter at the fixture clock's time, so no re-timing is needed. The malformed variant is hand-authored.
- Positions are COMPUTED by this application (SGP4 propagation of mean elements), not tracked or reported positions. Typical error is about 1 km at the element epoch and grows by roughly 1–3 km per day for low orbits; after a manoeuvre, or for objects with old elements, the error can be far larger.
- Element sets older than 30 days, objects whose propagation fails and objects that have decayed are rejected.
- The stations group contains every catalogued object associated with the ISS and the Chinese Space Station (modules, visiting vehicles, released cubesats, debris), not only the stations themselves.
- Only four CelesTrak groups are loaded (stations, gnss, weather, resource); a satellite listed in two groups appears once in each layer.
- The marker shows the point on the ground directly below the satellite; altitude and speed are given as attributes.

### NASA JPL Center for Near Earth Object Studies (CNEOS) — Fireball and bolide reports (SSD/CNEOS Fireball Data API) (`jpl-fireballs`)

- **Coverage:** Global; The five years before the current date (the archive itself starts in 1988)
- **Upstream cadence:** Irregular – events are added days to weeks after they occur; polled every 6 h, flagged stale after 2 d
- **Confidence:** CNEOS publishes no per-event confidence, so none is shown. Severity is derived by this application from the estimated impact energy (kilotons of TNT).
- **Retention:** Events are kept for ten years after they occurred.
- **Licence notes:** The API documentation asks users to avoid concurrent requests and to check the signature/version of each response. Credit NASA/JPL-Caltech (CNEOS).
- **Bundled fixture:** Complete, unmodified response of https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/fireball.api?date-min=2021-11-11&req-loc=true captured from the live API (173 events, newest 2026-10-04). The malformed variant is hand-authored.
- Not a complete record: only events reported by U.S. Government sensors are included, and reporting is delayed by days to weeks.
- Location is the point of peak brightness, reported to 0.1 degree; many events have no reported location and are omitted (req-loc=true).
- Impact energy is an estimate derived by CNEOS from the radiated energy with an empirical relation.

### The Space Devs — Launch Library 2 – upcoming orbital launches (`launch-library`)

- **Coverage:** Global launch sites; The next 50 scheduled launches (typically several weeks ahead); past launches persist locally until retention expires
- **Upstream cadence:** Continuously maintained by volunteers; schedules change frequently close to launch; polled every 1 h, flagged stale after 6 h
- **Confidence:** Confidence is derived by this application from Launch Library's own status (Go / To Be Confirmed / To Be Determined) and the stated precision of the launch time. It describes schedule certainty only.
- **Retention:** Launch entries are kept for 30 days after their launch window closed.
- **Licence notes:** The Space Devs FAQ: "You are free to use the data in any way, shape, or form, and share what you create with it. Please refrain from forwarding it without adding value." Attribution is encouraged, not mandatory. The free tier allows 15 API calls per hour per IP address, so this adapter makes one call per hour. Images referenced by the API carry their own licences and are not used.
- **Bundled fixture:** Captured from the live Launch Library 2 API (launches/upcoming, limit=50, mode=normal) and trimmed to the first 45 launches. To stay within the size budget, image/infographic/logo/program/agency sub-objects and long location descriptions were removed; every field the adapter reads is as captured. `next` was set to null. Launch times lie after the fixture epoch by nature (these are scheduled launches); last_updated values are all earlier than the epoch. The malformed variant is hand-authored.
- Schedules are community-maintained estimates; dates marked with month, quarter or year precision are placeholders.
- Only the next 50 launches are requested (free-tier rate limit of 15 calls per hour).
- The marker is the launch pad; it says nothing about the trajectory or the payload's destination.

### NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center (SWPC) — SWPC data services – OVATION aurora nowcast and space weather alerts, watches and warnings (`noaa-swpc`)

- **Coverage:** Global (aurora grid 1° × 1°; alerts are planet-wide bulletins without a location).; Aurora: the latest 30–90 minute forecast. Alerts: messages issued during the last 7 days (the file itself holds about a month).
- **Upstream cadence:** Aurora forecast about every 5 minutes; alerts as issued; polled every 10 min, flagged stale after 1 h
- **Confidence:** Neither product carries a confidence value, so none is assigned. Alert severity is derived by this application from the NOAA Space Weather Scale level (G/S/R 1–5) when the message states one: level 1 (Minor) → 1, 2 (Moderate) → 2, 3 (Strong) → 3, 4 (Severe) → 3, 5 (Extreme) → 4; messages without a scale level have no severity. Aurora cells carry the model probability as their metric and no severity.
- **Retention:** Aurora cells are updated in place and purged 1 day after their last forecast; alert bulletins are kept for 30 days.
- **Licence notes:** The SWPC data-access page lists services.swpc.noaa.gov/json and /products as the public data service and states no separate licence; the site links to the NOAA/NWS disclaimer, under which NWS web information is in the public domain unless noted otherwise and must not be presented as official material after modification.
- **Bundled fixture:** Both files were captured from the live services.swpc.noaa.gov on 2026-11-10. ovation_aurora_latest.json (920 KB, 65,160 cells) was trimmed to two contiguous latitude bands that contain both auroral ovals – 60°N…80°N and 88°S…48°S, all 360 longitudes, 22,320 cells, values untouched – and re-serialised compactly with the envelope (Observation Time, Forecast Time, Data Format, type) intact. Snapshot fixture: Observation Time was re-timed from 2026-11-10T17:43:00Z to 2026-11-10T23:52:00Z (eight minutes before the fixture epoch) and Forecast Time by the same offset from 18:26:00Z to 2026-11-11T00:35:00Z – the forecast's valid time is by nature 43 minutes after the observation and therefore falls after the epoch; the adapter uses the Observation Time as the record time. Geomagnetic activity was low at capture (maximum probability 21 %). alerts.json is the complete, unmodified capture (69 messages from 2026-10-11 to 2026-11-10 05:01 UTC, 21 of them within the 7 days before the epoch); no re-timing was needed. The two malformed variants were authored by hand.
- The aurora layer is a model forecast (OVATION Prime driven by solar-wind measurements at L1), not an observation of visible aurora; visibility also depends on darkness, cloud and local viewing conditions.
- To keep the layer light, only grid cells with a probability of at least 10 % are kept and the grid is thinned to every second degree of longitude and latitude, so the mapped oval is a 2° sample of the 1° product.
- Alerts are global bulletins with no geographic footprint; they are listed, not mapped. Only the title line and a short excerpt are stored – consult SWPC for the authoritative text.
- The JSON products are operational files without a formal schema or stability guarantee.

### Natural Earth — Natural Earth 1:50m cultural vectors – populated places and breakaway / disputed areas (`natural-earth`)

- **Coverage:** Global, generalised for 1:50 million scale; Static reference data, revised with Natural Earth releases (5.x)
- **Upstream cadence:** Occasional Natural Earth releases (months to years apart); polled every 30 d, flagged stale after 180 d
- **Confidence:** No per-record confidence is published. Volunteer-maintained cartographic reference data intended for small-scale maps; positions of cities are reliable to a few kilometres, disputed-area outlines are indicative only.
- **Retention:** Reference data: a feature is kept for as long as the current file lists it.
- **Licence notes:** "All versions of Natural Earth raster + vector map data found on this website are in the public domain." No permission or credit is required. Files are fetched from the project's official GitHub repository (nvkelso/natural-earth-vector).
- **Bundled fixture:** Captured from the official Natural Earth GitHub repository (nvkelso/natural-earth-vector, master, VERSION 5.2.0-pre). ne_50m_admin_0_breakaway_disputed_areas.geojson is the complete, unmodified file (28 areas, 142 KB; no vertex decimation was needed). ne_50m_populated_places_simple.geojson was trimmed from 1,251 to 450 features: every national capital and regional capital class (243), 6 Antarctic scientific stations, the one historic place and the most populous remaining cities; kept features are byte-for-byte value-identical but the file was re-serialised one feature per line with the envelope (name, crs, bbox) intact. Together about 450 KB, above the usual 400 KB budget and within the per-file sizes requested for this adapter. Reference data without timestamps. The malformed variants were authored by hand.
- Boundaries and names of disputed areas are cartographic depictions by Natural Earth, which shows de-facto control; they are NOT an endorsement of any territorial claim by this application, and other authorities draw them differently.

- Outlines are generalised for 1:50 million scale and are unsuitable for measuring or for locating a point relative to a line of control.

### Peter Bird (UCLA) / Hugo Ahlenius, Nordpil — PB2002 plate boundary model (Bird, 2003) – GeoJSON conversion (fraxen/tectonicplates) (`tectonic-plates`)

- **Coverage:** Global; Static scientific model published in 2003 (boundaries digitised 1987–2002); converted to GeoJSON in 2014
- **Upstream cadence:** Static; the repository changes very rarely; polled every 30 d, flagged stale after 365 d
- **Confidence:** No per-record confidence is published. Peer-reviewed global model (Bird 2003); each segment carries the name of the publication or digitisation it was taken from.
- **Retention:** Reference data: a segment is kept for as long as the published file lists it.
- **Licence notes:** The repository README and LICENSE.md state: "This collection is made available under the Open Data Commons Attribution License" and ask that Hugo Ahlenius, Nordpil and Peter Bird be credited. (It is ODC-By, not the public-domain ODC PDDL.) The underlying model is from Bird (2003), G-cubed, doi:10.1029/2001GC000252.
- **Bundled fixture:** PB2002_boundaries.json is the complete, unmodified file captured from github.com/fraxen/tectonicplates (master): 241 boundary segments, 6,292 vertices, 226 KB – no vertex decimation was needed. Static reference data without timestamps. The malformed variant was authored by hand.
- A scientific model from 2003, not an observation: boundary positions are interpretations, many plate boundaries are in reality broad deformation zones (Bird's 'orogens') rather than sharp lines, and offshore segments are accurate to tens of kilometres at best.
- Only the boundary type 'subduction' is flagged in this conversion; ridges, transforms and other classes of the original model are not distinguished.
- Segments that cross the 180° meridian were split by the converter; a few segments are duplicated in the file.
- Proximity to a plate boundary says nothing by itself about current seismic or volcanic hazard.

### UNESCO World Heritage Centre — World Heritage List (UNESCO Datahub dataset whc001) (`unesco-world-heritage`)

- **Coverage:** Global (properties in about 170 States Parties); Current List; inscriptions since 1978. Changes follow the annual session of the World Heritage Committee.
- **Upstream cadence:** Annual (after each World Heritage Committee session), with occasional corrections; polled every 30 d, flagged stale after 180 d
- **Confidence:** No per-record confidence is published. Authoritative register maintained by the UNESCO World Heritage Centre. Severity 2 is assigned by this application to properties that UNESCO itself flags as being on the List of World Heritage in Danger.
- **Retention:** Reference data: a property is kept for as long as UNESCO lists it; delisted properties disappear with the next refresh.
- **Licence notes:** Licence as declared in the dataset metadata on data.unesco.org (license: CC BY-SA 4.0, publisher UNESCO, reference https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/). ShareAlike: adaptations of the data that are redistributed must carry the same licence. Only a short excerpt (at most 300 characters) of each description is stored; site images are not used.
- **Bundled fixture:** Captured from the UNESCO Datahub (Opendatasoft Explore API v2.1, dataset whc001, exports/json with the adapter's select list; dataset last processed 2026-11-08, 1,273 properties, 1.27 MB) and trimmed to 359 properties without altering any kept record: all 58 properties on the List of World Heritage in Danger, the three properties with missing coordinates or missing State Party, a few well-known sites, and an even sample across every UNESCO region and category. The file was re-serialised one record per line. Descriptions are UNESCO's short descriptions (CC BY-SA 4.0); the adapter stores at most 300 characters of each. Reference data without per-record timestamps (inscription years only). The malformed variant was authored by hand.
- One reference point per property: serial and transboundary properties (routes, groups of monuments, large parks) extend far beyond that point, and two serial properties are published without any coordinates and are attached to their State(s) Party instead.
- The 'in danger' flag is the formal List of World Heritage in Danger decided by the Committee once a year; it is not a real-time damage or threat assessment, and sites under acute threat may not (yet) be listed.
- The States listed are the States Parties that nominated the property, as published by UNESCO; this is not a statement about sovereignty (the Old City of Jerusalem is published without a State Party).
- Open-data API quota: 10,000 requests per day per client; the dataset itself is refreshed about once a year.
